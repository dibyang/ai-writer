# 易懂性、反烧脑与术语可读性审稿规则

Use this module when the user mentions 易懂性, 太烧脑, 看不懂, 普通读者, 专业名词, 术语首次出现, 名词解释, 隐藏线索, 信息密度, or when a manuscript is clever but cognitively tiring.

## Core Principle

A reader may fail to solve the mystery, but should not fail to understand what the chapter is asking them to worry about.

Do not simplify away genre complexity. Instead, ensure each chapter has an accessible surface line while deeper clues remain layered.

## Three-Layer Information Rule

Separate chapter information into three layers:

| Layer | Reader must understand now? | Contents | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surface line | Yes | What happened, who is endangered, what worsened, why continue | Reader only feels atmosphere, cleverness, or complexity |
| Inference line | Partly | Why an object, wound, time, wording, or behavior is suspicious | Clue has no visible function until later explanation |
| Hidden line | Not yet | Long-arc identity, fate, conspiracy, closed-loop, deep motive | Hidden line steals attention from the chapter event |

Hard rule: if the surface line is unclear, the chapter fails readability even if the puzzle is logically fair.

## Five Ordinary-Reader Questions

For each chapter or scene under review, check whether an ordinary reader can answer:

1. What happened here?
2. Who is now in danger, less credible, trapped, erased, or worse off?
3. What is the visible function of the key clue, term, record, object, or wound?
4. What changed by the end: person, evidence, trust, access, time, power, or story of death?
5. Why should the reader continue?

If the answer is only "the evidence chain is more complex" or "the mystery deepened", flag readability risk.

## First-Appearance Term Rule

Professional, historical, procedural, magical, institutional, medical, legal, forensic, technical, or invented terms must be clear on first meaningful use.

For first appearances, require at least one of:

- action explanation: a character uses, opens, signs, seals, measures, extracts, or points to it;
- consequence explanation: it ties someone to an act, blocks a route, changes credibility, creates liability, or saves/harms someone;
- one plain sentence: what it is and what it does, without encyclopedia exposition;
- local note/appendix: detailed background outside the publication-facing scene.

A hidden clue may be underestimated, but its basic function must be readable. Do not hide clues by making readers unable to parse terminology.

## Anti-Overload Checks

Flag and recommend reducing load when a chapter does several of these at once:

- introduces three or more important names;
- introduces two or more technical/institutional terms;
- introduces a new object, document, location, and time rule together;
- advances mainly through reports, screens, ledgers, archives, annotations, classifications, or records;
- resolves or reinterprets two or more old clues in one scene;
- requires memory of multiple prior cases before the current danger makes sense.

Recommended fixes:

- state the surface danger before the technical reasoning;
- let action or consequence explain the term;
- make one clue primary and demote others to supporting checks;
- insert a scene-native recap: three known facts, two endangered people, one next question;
- repeat a light clue handle when a term returns after a long gap.

## Hidden-Clue Fairness

A fair hidden clue should:

- have a visible ordinary function on first read;
- attach to a concrete object, action, body detail, status change, or emotional reaction;
- be memorable enough that the reveal feels earned;
- allow the reader to say "I saw it, but misread its importance."

Unfair or too-burn-brain clues:

- rely on obscure terminology with no surface explanation;
- require later exposition before the current chapter matters;
- require the reader to hold too many old threads without a current-scene handle;
- feel like author-only bookkeeping.

## Review Output Add-On

When this module applies, include a short readability judgment:

- Surface line: pass / caution / fail.
- Term clarity: pass / caution / fail.
- Hidden clue fairness: pass / caution / fail.
- Cognitive load: pass / caution / fail.
- Highest-yield fix: one or two actions, preferably scene-native rather than expository.

Use this module alongside suspense and page-turner review. A chapter can be smart and publishable but still need readability work before it can be a strong page-turner.
