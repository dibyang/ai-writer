# 历史小说与古代社会小说审稿规则


Apply `psychology-readability.md` whenever historical institutions, status, ritual, family law, court rules, gender constraints, documents, punishment, or reputation pressure decide what a character can say or do. The reader must feel how history enters the body and choice.
Apply `general-review.md` first. Apply this file for 历史小说、古代言情、朝堂、宫廷、官场、家族、战争、年代、架空古代、历史悬疑、古代职业文等。If the plot uses cases or clues, also apply `suspense-review.md`. If there is time travel or modern/ancient contrast, also apply `transmigration-review.md` and/or `dual-world-review.md`.

## Core Standard

历史感不是堆称谓、器物和制度名，而是让读者看见那个时代如何限制人的选择、改变关系、决定生死和名声。

## Period Readability

Professional or historical detail must be understandable through consequence.

When titles, ranks, laws, rituals, household rules, clans, land, military systems, marriage systems, documents, taxes, household registration, curfew, or punishments appear, readers must know:

- who has power;
- who is trapped;
- what can be lost;
- what action becomes impossible;
- why this rule matters now.

Avoid dense name/title lists without immediate dramatic function.

## Historical Texture Checks

1. Food, clothing, housing, transport, lighting, medicine, money, weapons, documents, gender rules, and etiquette should match the chosen period or declared alternate setting.
2. Institutions should constrain plot, not just decorate it.
3. Characters should not all speak with modern assumptions unless the story has a reason.
4. Historical constraints should create plot pressure: reputation, clan law, official rank, household control, travel limits, literacy, communication delay, evidence standards, property rights.
5. Exposition should enter through action, conflict, or consequence.

## Character in History Rule

Do not flatten historical characters into either modern mouthpieces or ignorant foils.

Check:

- Does each major character have a worldview shaped by their time?
- Does the protagonist understand, resist, exploit, or misunderstand the rules?
- Do lower-status characters face different costs from elites?
- Are women, servants, soldiers, clerks, artisans, monks, merchants, and officials constrained differently?

## Political and Family Logic

For court, officialdom, clan, palace, or military stories, review:

- interest chains: who benefits from each outcome;
- document chains: who can write, seal, erase, archive, or reinterpret records;
- reputation chains: who loses face, marriage prospects, office, land, safety;
- violence chains: who can legally punish whom;
- silence chains: who cannot speak even when they know truth.

## Historical Suspense Add-On

When historical setting carries a case:

- evidence standards must fit the period;
- documents and testimony can be weapons;
- the protagonist cannot use modern proof methods unless the premise grants them;
- ordinary readers must understand why period law/procedure can distort truth.

## Language Rule

Keep period flavor without sacrificing understanding. Use some historical texture in nouns, rituals, address, and worldview, but write stakes in clear modern-readable Chinese. If readers must stop to translate a phrase when danger should be immediate, revise.
