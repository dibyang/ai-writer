# 陌生读者追读与爆款阅读感审稿规则

Apply `general-review.md` first. Use this file when reviewing long chapter batches, middle-to-late arcs, formal release candidates, stranger-reader feedback, reader stickiness, binge-read fatigue, commercial readability, or 爆款/追读/上头/陌生读者口径.

## Core Purpose

A manuscript can be logically complete and still feel cold. This module catches problems where the story is clever, coherent, and professionally textured, but ordinary readers begin to feel they are reading a system explanation rather than watching people under pressure.

Judge this separately from basic publishability when useful:

- Formal release line: structure, logic, fairness, prose, closure.
- Page-turner line: lived danger, emotional attachment, scene pressure, reader hunger, and whether answers become sharper conflict rather than tidy explanation.

If the user asks for 爆款感, 追读, 陌生第三方读者, or commercial readability, severe failures in this module are hard blockers for that goal even when the manuscript can technically publish.

## 1. Concept Must Land In Scene Rule

Abstract suspense terms must be anchored in visible pressure. When a scene advances through terms such as 证据链, 案牍, 复核, 报告, 屏幕, 待复核, 归类, 标注, 删改, 封存, 程序, or 权限, check whether the same scene also contains at least one concrete reader-gripping pressure:

- a person is taken away, trapped, searched, questioned, silenced, exposed, or forced to choose;
- an object is seized, hidden, broken, sealed, moved, misread, or almost lost;
- a door, footstep, lamp, weapon, official hand, document, body, wound, or physical setting increases danger;
- a character leaks fear, hesitation, anger, shame, nausea, trembling, insomnia, appetite loss, or delayed shock;
- the abstract rule immediately changes who can live, speak, be believed, keep a name, keep a job, or stay out of custody.

Hard warning: if a climax happens mainly through explanation, screen display, report status, archival wording, or abstract inference, require scene pressure before calling it page-turner strong.

## 2. Fourth-Quarter Clarity Rule

For late-middle arcs where the plot becomes conceptually sophisticated, especially arcs about drafts, original texts, annotations, official copies, reports, evidence chains, method misuse, or institutional classification, the review must repeatedly pull the problem back to the plain danger.

Every 2-3 chapters, an ordinary reader should be able to answer:

- Who is about to die, be arrested, lose a name, lose credibility, be framed, or be erased?
- What will happen in the next scene if the protagonist is wrong?
- Which living person pays if this document, report, method, or record is mishandled?
- What does the antagonist gain now, not just in the abstract system?

If readers understand how the system works but stop fearing what it will do to someone immediately, the arc is too clever and too cold.

## 3. Final-Arc Live Crisis Rule

A final arc must not become a checklist of answers. Each reveal should function as a blade, not only a key.

Check whether every major answer increases a live crisis:

- the protagonist loses authority, freedom, trust, access, identity, or professional position;
- an ally becomes easier to prosecute, erase, frame, or sacrifice;
- the love/trust relationship becomes harder, not merely clearer;
- the evidence that solves one mystery makes another person less safe;
- the final truth forces an irreversible choice in the present scene.

Hard blocker for final-arc page-turner review: if chapters primarily proceed as one reveal per chapter, with danger already over and only explanations remaining, mark the ending as settlement-list fatigue even if every answer is logical.

## 4. Anti-Summary Sentence Rhythm Rule

Cognitive reversal sentences are powerful but must be rationed. Flag overuse of patterns such as:

- 她终于明白...
- 她忽然明白...
- 她这才知道...
- 不是 X, 而是 Y...
- 这一次...
- 原来...
- 真正的...不是..., 是...

These can stay at major recognition points. At ordinary chapter turns, prefer action, object state, silence, body reaction, an unfinished line, or a changed relationship. Let readers arrive at the conclusion through scene pressure instead of having the narration summarize every turn.

If multiple consecutive chapter endings rely on explanatory recognition, downgrade pacing and prose texture.

## 5. Protagonist Sticky Emotion Rule

A capable protagonist must not only be admirable. Readers also need moments that make them worry for her, ache for her, or want to stay with her after the case logic is clear.

For high-pressure chapters, check for small private residue:

- cannot sleep, cannot eat, forgets a message, avoids a call, washes hands too long, misses a familiar routine;
- hand tremor, nausea, cold fingertips, sore throat, delayed tears, anger that cannot be used;
- fear that professional judgment is becoming contamination;
- fear of becoming part of the same evidence system she is trying to challenge;
- one private sentence she cannot say aloud because it would break professional control.

Do not add melodrama. The best residue is small, concrete, and behavior-changing.

## 6. Important Male Lead / Ally Humanity Rule

A sacrificial ally cannot only know, endure, withhold, and bear consequences. If a character repeatedly functions as protector, secret keeper, guide, or sacrifice device, require private human moments that do not only serve case mechanics.

Check for at least some of these across late arcs:

- one selfish wish;
- one fear he does not want the protagonist to see;
- one moment of weakness, tiredness, hesitation, or resentment;
- one small ordinary preference or memory;
- one choice where he is not perfectly noble, but still human.

If the character always acts as fate's instrument, downgrade emotional impact of sacrifice.

## 7. Modern-Line Human Cost Rule

Modern procedural characters should not only check, block, remind, report, or grant access. In a dual-world/procedural novel, modern-line people also need moments of agency and risk.

Check whether at least one modern ally, colleague, superior, or investigator:

- bends a procedure and pays or risks a cost;
- protects the protagonist while still doubting her;
- chooses a hard official wording that changes the case;
- takes responsibility for a decision rather than only requesting more review;
- loses access, reputation, sleep, trust, or safety because of helping.

If modern characters only represent process, the modern line feels stable but emotionally thin.

## 8. Blockbuster Final-Draft Dual Score Rule

For full manuscripts, final volumes, or any review where the target is 爆款/追读/上头, give two separate judgments:

- `正式发布线`: can the manuscript be released without structural, logic, fairness, continuity, or prose blockers?
- `爆款追读线`: will a cold third-party reader keep reading because they fear for people, care about relationships, and feel each answer cut into a living person?

Do not let a strong evidence chain inflate the page-turner score. A manuscript with complete logic but cold emotional delivery should be described as `不阻止发布，但不建议直接作为最终爆款稿`.

## 9. Final-Volume System Density Gate

For final volumes, count or at least actively inspect system/procedure language such as 复核, 报告, 屏幕, 证据链, 案牍, 归类, 标注, 删改, 封存, 程序, 权限, 档案, 记录.

High density is not automatically bad, but it becomes a page-turner risk when the dominant reading experience is `watching a system reconcile accounts` rather than `watching people get hurt, choose, lose, protect, or betray`.

For each final-volume reveal, require at least one immediate living-person cost:

- someone loses access, credibility, name, job, safety, freedom, family protection, or legal standing;
- someone becomes easier to prosecute, erase, expose, frame, or sacrifice;
- someone must choose between truth and protecting another person;
- the evidence saves one dead person while endangering one living person;
- the protagonist's authority or self-image is damaged in a way that affects the next scene.

If a chapter can only be summarized as `the evidence chain became more complete`, mark the chapter below the 爆款追读线 even if it passes formal release.

## 10. Continuous Protagonist Damage Rule

A capable protagonist must show cumulative wear, not only isolated body reactions at plot nodes. For late arcs and final volumes, check whether the protagonist's pressure leaves behavioral residue across scenes:

- avoids a phone call, message, doorway, file, name, or familiar place;
- sleeps badly, wakes with an old reflex, eats less, washes hands too long, or pauses before touching professional tools;
- cannot answer a family member, victim's relative, colleague, or ally directly;
- treats her own professional judgment as possible contamination;
- loses access, procedural authority, trust, or certainty, and that loss changes how she acts next.

Do not ask for more explanatory inner monologue. The preferred fix is repeated small behavior that shows the case is wearing her down.

## 11. Sacrificial Ally Non-Tool Gate

For a major ally or male lead who repeatedly knows, withholds, protects, sacrifices, or bears consequences, require human imperfection before the final sacrifice lands.

Across late arcs, look for at least one of:

- a selfish wish;
- fear he does not want the protagonist to see;
- fatigue, resentment, hesitation, or a moment of wanting to stop;
- a small ordinary preference or memory unrelated to case mechanics;
- a choice that is protective but not perfectly noble;
- a moment where he almost lets someone else bear the cost.

If the character only functions as secret keeper, shield, guide, and sacrifice device, downgrade emotional impact even if the plot role is elegant.

## 12. Every Two-to-Three Chapters Living-Danger Check

In concept-heavy arcs, every two or three chapters must let ordinary readers answer immediately:

- Who may be arrested, erased, discredited, silenced, framed, or killed now?
- If the protagonist is wrong, who pays in the next scene?
- Which living person is endangered by preserving or exposing this evidence?
- What does the antagonist gain now, in concrete human terms?

If readers can only answer `the system/report/record changed`, the arc is too cold for 爆款追读.

## 13. Explosive Final Chapter Gate

For novels targeting 爆款, 追读, 上头, or final market-facing release, the last chapter or final reveal chapter must not read like sequential case confirmation, catalog reconciliation, archive checkout, or a clean list of solved mysteries.

A final chapter fails the 爆款追读线 if its dominant structure is: one old case is named, then another old case is named, then another old case is named, while the present-tense danger has already ended and no living person is forced to make a new irreversible choice.

Require at least one present-tense final-chapter pressure that ordinary readers can feel without understanding the whole evidence system:

- the protagonist loses a real job function, permission, professional role, relationship, legal standing, or future access in the scene;
- a modern ally must sign, testify, be listed, accept inquiry, or otherwise put their own name under risk;
- a living person must remain protected by withholding a satisfying answer, name, confession, or public vindication;
- the protagonist must refuse an emotionally tempting fix because it would endanger people the sacrifice was meant to protect;
- the final action changes how the protagonist can work in the next ordinary case, not only how the old mystery is archived.

Hard warning: a beautiful, logical final chapter is still below 爆款终稿线 if readers mainly feel that every answer has been filed correctly. The final answer must also cost someone something now.

## 14. Stop Proving Cleverness Gate

For 爆款 or market-facing reviews, do not let clever structure, perfect evidence-chain closure, or beautiful thematic symmetry hide a cold reading experience.

Flag the manuscript when the reviewer's strongest praise is mainly "the structure is self-consistent", "the evidence chain closes", "the system explains itself", or "the design is smart", but it is hard to name who the reader is hurting for, angry for, or unwilling to leave.

Hard questions:

- Does the evidence chain feel as if it wins by itself, or do people visibly pay for making it hold?
- Can a reader describe the final emotional wound without using technical words like 复核, 归档, 证据链, 标注, or 程序?
- Does each major answer make readers think "this is correct, but I cannot stand what it costs"?
- Is the final pleasure only intellectual closure, or also attachment, grief, unfairness, and reluctance to say goodbye?

If the book is already smart enough, the highest-yield review direction is usually not more explanation. Push the draft toward pain, reluctance, human imperfection, and immediate consequence.

## 15. Continuous Professional Damage Chain

A protagonist who is defined by a profession must not only suffer emotionally; the story should show how the case changes their professional reflexes.

For late arcs and final chapters, look for a chain of altered behavior, not isolated reaction tags:

- the protagonist sees an original object or body and first restrains their hand;
- checks whether their name is in the correct permission/signature/role column before acting;
- no longer signs, touches, speaks, or promises in the old automatic way;
- enters a new ordinary case through a changed role, limited access, a witness position, or a delayed professional gesture;
- corrects or suppresses an old professional instinct because the case has made it dangerous.

If the manuscript only gives hand tremors, throat tightness, cold fingers, or one symbolic loss of authority, mark the professional damage as incomplete for 爆款终稿. The reader should feel that the protagonist cannot simply win and return to the same job-self.

## 16. Imperfect Sacrifice Micro-Moment Gate

A perfectly correct sacrifice earns respect; an imperfect sacrifice earns ache. For a major ally or male lead whose arc depends on taking blame, losing a name, withholding truth, or protecting others, require at least one brief humanly untidy moment near the sacrifice.

Acceptable micro-moments include:

- a flash of disappointment when the protagonist does not rescue or expose him;
- a selfish wish that she would choose him before choosing the safer truth;
- fatigue, resentment, or fear that leaks before he resumes control;
- a moment where he almost lets another person share the burden;
- a small ordinary desire for morning, food, rest, touch, a name, or being remembered;
- a response that is not noble first, but becomes restraint after a beat.

Do not require melodramatic confession. The moment can be tiny, but it must prove he is not only a fate device, shield, or perfectly designed sacrifice.

## 17. Modern Emotional Core Gate

When a modern case or modern family member carries emotional ballast, do not let the result stop at "疑点保留", "复核方向采纳", or "补充追问". For 爆款追读, the chapter should also show what that limited procedural result means to a living person.

Check whether the scene gives ordinary readers a human handle:

- a family member's breath, silence, hand, photo, repeated question, or inability to celebrate;
- the protagonist's inability to promise a result even after preserving the question;
- a modern ally taking responsibility for keeping the question alive;
- a clear sense that the win is partial, unsatisfying, and still necessary.

If the modern emotional core only produces a cleaner report status, mark it as too cold even if the procedure is correct.

## 18. Review Output Add-On

When this module applies, include a short page-turner judgment. For full manuscripts or final volumes, include both formal release and blockbuster/page-turner final-draft judgments:

1. Formal release verdict: pass / block / not applicable.
2. 爆款追读 verdict: pass / caution / blocker.
3. Concept density: pass / caution / blocker.
4. Live scene pressure: pass / caution / blocker.
5. Character stickiness: pass / caution / blocker.
6. Final-arc crisis: pass / caution / blocker, if relevant.
7. One or two highest-yield fixes.

Do not confuse a cold-but-logical manuscript with a broken manuscript. State whether the issue blocks formal publication, blocks page-turner/commercial goal, or is only a polishing opportunity.
## 9. External Cold-Read Score Rule

For 爆款/追读/上头 reviews, do not reuse the internal publication-gate score as the page-turner market score. The page-turner score is a stranger-reader risk estimate: it asks whether readers care, fear, ache, and continue, not merely whether the manuscript satisfies craft rules.

Use this scale:

- 95+: readers are likely to feel strong hunger and emotional attachment; call it strong page-turner pass.
- 90-94: page-turner pass with remaining ceiling risk.
- 85-89: publishable and promising, but not a final blockbuster draft.
- 80-84: formal publication may pass, but market stickiness is unstable.
- Below 80: unsuitable for a blockbuster target without major market-facing revision.

If the best praise is "closed loop, clever evidence, complete rules", with no clear answer to "who will readers hurt for", withhold 90+. If readers can admire the system but not miss the people, the page-turner score must stay below the strong-pass range.
