# 悬疑与案件小说审稿规则


Apply `psychology-readability.md` whenever suspense depends on fear, shock, betrayal, evidence reversal, identity danger, institutional pressure, or a chapter-ending hook. A clue reveal must also be emotionally and cognitively readable, not only logically correct.
Apply `general-review.md` first. Then apply this file for suspense, mystery, crime, thriller, forensic, clue-based, case-based, investigative, or puzzle-driven fiction.

## Suspense Standard

The central question is not whether the atmosphere is strange, but whether readers keep wanting the answer.

Good suspense gives readers the feeling: I know a little, but not enough, so I must continue.

## Scoring Dimensions

Use these dimensions when scoring suspense:

| Item | Weight | Focus |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Core suspense | 15 | opening hook, main question, sustained pull |
| Case logic | 20 | motive, timeline, evidence chain, reasoning |
| Clue design | 15 | foreshadowing, fairness, misdirection, payoff |
| Character | 15 | protagonist, suspects, culprit, victim weight |
| Pacing | 10 | chapter movement, information gain, climax timing |
| Twist quality | 10 | surprise, logic, emotional consequence |
| Atmosphere | 5 | scene pressure, distinct setting, mood serving plot |
| Theme depth | 10 | human/social/emotional residue |

## Hard Checks

Block publication if any of these are true:

- The main mystery is vague or only atmospheric.
- Key evidence appears for the first time at the ending.
- The solution depends on coincidence, author concealment, or sudden inspiration.
- The culprit, motive, or timeline cannot stand.
- The protagonist hides known crucial facts from readers without a fair narrative reason.
- Readers cannot participate in inference.
- A twist only shocks but does not deepen event, character, or emotion.

## Clue Rules

A good clue should look normal on first read and become important after truth appears.

Check:

- true clues, red herrings, and background noise coexist;
- clues are embedded in ordinary scenes;
- important clues are reinforced enough to be remembered;
- misdirection is fair, not cheating;
- all major clues are eventually explained;
- the reveal lets readers say: the text warned me, but I missed it.

## Fairness Rules

Authors may mislead readers, but must not cheat them.

Unfair moves include:

- protagonist already knows the truth but narration withholds it;
- key action is deliberately omitted;
- dream, hallucination, or insanity explains everything without fair setup;
- ending says previous events were fake without enough prior contract;
- professional conclusion appears without process.

## Protagonist Error Rule

An expert protagonist may be correct professionally but must sometimes be wrong socially, institutionally, emotionally, or strategically. If every professional observation immediately saves people and solves the case, suspense flattens.

Check whether the protagonist pays a cost for correct evidence but wrong timing, wrong trust, wrong disclosure, or wrong understanding of local power.
