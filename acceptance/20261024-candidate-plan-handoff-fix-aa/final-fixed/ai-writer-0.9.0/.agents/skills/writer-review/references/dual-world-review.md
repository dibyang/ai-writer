# 双时空与异世界调查审稿规则


Apply `psychology-readability.md` for every first entry, return, identity/body mismatch, impossible evidence discovery, rule shock, or moment where the protagonist cannot safely treat events as a dream. Dual-world mechanics must be felt through body, cognition, and choice.
Apply `general-review.md` first. If the story is also suspense/case-based, apply `suspense-review.md` before this file.
For full architecture, setting-bible, time-loop, body-state, or cross-world object review, also apply `architecture-continuity-review.md`.

## Core Principle

Dual timelines, dream entry, transmigration, parallel worlds, modern/ancient cases, or two-world investigation must interlock. They cannot be two separate novels alternating chapters.

## Dual-Line Verification Rule

Modern line and other-world line must provide evidence to each other.

Check:

- Does a clue in one world change decisions in the other?
- Does one line correct a false assumption in the other?
- Does the modern evidence explain old events, and old events open modern questions?
- If either line were removed, would the mystery collapse? It should.

## Entry Mechanism Rule

The entry mechanism must be stable enough for readers to trust stakes.

Check:

- The protagonist cannot enter or exit purely at convenience.
- Waking/sleeping/returning has cost, delay, uncertainty, bodily residue, memory limits, permission limits, or evidence consequences.
- The story does not use dream logic to erase danger.
- The protagonist may not be able to confirm whether it is real, but must have concrete reasons not to test it recklessly.

## Mirror Case Rule

The two worlds should echo through theme, method, relationship, institution, object, wound, document, or body.

Good echoes include:

- similar wound with different context;
- ancient record and modern report both distorting a death;
- one victim teaching how to read another;
- a historical object becoming modern evidence;
- a modern procedure showing why an ancient document is dangerous.

## Identity and Body Anchor

When the protagonist appears in another world/body/status, the text must anchor:

- what body she is in;
- what clothing, token, accent, injury, or status others see;
- what identity is temporary cover versus legally/socially recognized identity;
- what would happen if that identity fails.

## Ordinary Reader Standard

Historical systems, official titles, night bans, documents, household rules, or legal categories must be readable through consequences. Readers need not know the institution, but must know how it can trap, punish, silence, or rewrite someone.
