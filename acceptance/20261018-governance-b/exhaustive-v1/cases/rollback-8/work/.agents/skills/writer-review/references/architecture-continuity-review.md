# 小说架构连续性与规则治理审查

Apply `general-review.md` first. Use this module for 小说架构、全书大纲、设定集、规则库、时间轴、双时空、循环因果、闭环物、物证链、长线伏笔、实现约束、发布审核体系和多文档一致性审查。

## Core Standard

架构审查不是继续增加设定，而是确认已有设定能被稳定写成场景。任何规则都必须回答：谁在什么时刻知道它，角色怎样触发它，它怎样改变可选行动，读者凭什么相信它没有临时变形。

## 1. Canonical Source Hierarchy

规则密集型项目必须建立单一事实源和覆盖顺序。至少区分：

1. 世界运行与不可违反规则；
2. 时间轴、地点、身体/身份和核心物件状态；
3. 全书结构与分卷功能；
4. 人物认知、伏笔和证据接触链；
5. 分场执行稿与章节正文；
6. 审核清单和历史变更记录。

Hard blocker:

- 同一问题在多个文档保留不同答案；
- 变更记录中的旧方案仍被执行稿当成现行规则；
- “以后再决定”的规则已经影响现有章节因果；
- 下位文档临场创造上位规则没有的例外。

Required output: name the canonical file or decision for every conflict. Do not merely say “需要统一”.

## 2. Operational Rule Closure

故事可以不解释宇宙成因，但必须解释角色可操作的边界。

For each extraordinary mechanism, check:

- trigger: what is necessary, what merely increases probability;
- entry/exit: where body, identity, possessions, witnesses, and injuries remain;
- time: elapsed-time ratio, missed windows, sleep/travel gaps, and calendar anchors;
- cost: bodily, relational, legal, professional, historical, or evidentiary loss;
- failure: what happens when the mechanism is interrupted, delayed, abused, or ends;
- exception: whether it is singular, prefigured, costly, and incapable of opening a reusable inventory.

Do not force a scientific or mystical explanation when the story's design is to preserve mystery. Separate `operationally knowable` from `cosmologically unanswered`.

## 3. Body, Identity, and Status Matrix

For body-swap, dream-entry, dual-world, rebirth, transmigration, clone, avatar, or identity-replacement stories, build a state matrix:

| State | Body/location | Consciousness | What observers see | Can be moved/harmed | Return consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Audit every transition for:

- physical location before and after;
- who can witness, carry, search, treat, restrain, or impersonate the body;
- clothing, tools, injuries, biological needs, and legal identity;
- whether return is being used as a no-cost escape;
- whether a missed entry leaves a body or social role unattended.

Hard blocker: the plot depends on a body disappearing, sleeping, surviving, or relocating differently in different chapters without an explicit rule.

## 4. Closed-Loop Object Lifecycle

Every time-loop or cross-world object needs one complete lifecycle table:

| Stage | Object state/age | Location | Custodian | Observed by whom | Irreversible change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Require:

- origin and lawful possession;
- first independent observation;
- every handoff, concealment, use, damage, burial, recovery, and modern re-observation;
- the exact scene where transfer across time/world occurs;
- a visible cost for the character who completes the loop;
- no unexplained copy, teleport, second exception, or pre-handoff possession by another character.

If young and old states of the same object coexist, both states must be distinguished by age, custody, marks, and observation records. The manuscript must not call them copies unless copying is genuinely the rule.

An object is not “necessary” merely because ancient/local tools are described as inferior. Its necessity must come from controlled operation, provenance, calibration, recognition, or cross-evidence value.

## 5. Evidence State Classes

For mysteries where actions in one timeline appear to affect evidence in another, classify evidence before judging continuity:

1. Observed facts: photographed, numbered, measured, archived, witnessed, or formally recorded. These cannot silently change.
2. Latent facts: covered, uncleaned, untranslated, unread, unopened, untested, or not yet independently observed. These may later be revealed.
3. Interpretations/status: report wording, attribution, legal classification, confidence level, access state, or scholarly reading. These may be revised through process.

Hard blockers:

- an archived physical fact changes to create a hook;
- a newly revealed latent fact is described as if history rewrote itself;
- a revised interpretation is confused with changing the object;
- the reader cannot tell whether the change is physical, informational, or procedural.

## 6. Timeline and Knowledge Ledger

Maintain at least three ledgers for long-form work:

- objective timeline: what physically happened and when;
- character knowledge timeline: who knows, assumes, hides, or misreads what;
- object/evidence custody timeline: where the item is and who can touch it.

For dual timelines, also record entry, exit, elapsed time, unattended time, body location, and caregiver/custodian. For historical fiction, fix the internal year/season early enough to stabilize daylight, travel, institutions, titles, warfare, ritual, and material culture.

Hard blocker: a solution requires a character to know information before acquisition, arrive without elapsed time, or use an object before custody.

## 7. Chapter Irreversible Change Rule

Do not require every chapter to make the situation worse. That produces one-note pressure and false cliffhangers.

Require instead:

> Every chapter must make at least one irreversible change to danger, evidence meaning, relationship, authority, time, resources, antagonist leverage, or character choice.

Across three consecutive chapters:

- at least two should increase pressure, cost, or urgency;
- one may deliver payoff, recovery, relationship movement, life texture, aftermath, or a limited win;
- the lower-pressure chapter must still change the next available action.

For a twelve-chapter commercial arc, a useful default is 3-4 high-pressure hooks, 5-6 medium hooks, and 2-3 quiet/aftermath hooks. Treat this as a diagnostic ratio, not a mandatory formula. Flag three consecutive hooks of the same type.

## 8. Visible Antagonist Loss and Survivor Payoff

Institutional evil can survive, but a named antagonist must lose something visible: office, money, client, freedom, status, credibility, protection, evidence control, or a scapegoat mechanism.

At each major case or arc ending, ask:

- What did the victim win back: name, cause of death, testimony, protected person, property, choice, or a crack in the lie?
- Which living person survived because of that choice?
- What later action proves the survivor was not merely an end-of-arc note?
- What did the antagonist irreversibly lose on-page?

Repeated “the lie did not fully close” endings are not enough for long-form commercial payoff.

When linking cases, prefer method inheritance, institutional imitation, shared channels, document language, or learned tactics over adding one mastermind behind every event.

## 9. Historical Institution Anchor

For historical or pseudo-historical stories, distinguish:

- formal rule;
- uneven enforcement;
- legal exception;
- privilege or corruption;
- local practice;
- deliberate fictional compression.

Fix an internal date/season when climate, curfew, office structure, travel, war, ritual, or technology depends on it. Do not let “historical atmosphere” substitute for operational facts. Record which sources support the boundary and which details are fictionalized.

## 10. Six Hard Release Gates

Use six hard gates for architecture-heavy release review. Any failure blocks final release:

1. Continuity: timeline, location, knowledge, injury, identity, custody, and evidence state agree.
2. Physical/professional operability: body actions, object handling, forensic/legal/historical procedures can happen as written.
3. Mystery fairness: major answers have prior clues and do not rely on a late rule.
4. Character logic: decisions fit personality, interests, trauma, knowledge, and immediate pressure.
5. Accessibility: ordinary readers can state what happened, who is in danger, and what to follow next.
6. Publication cleanliness: no draft markers, author/meta language, unresolved decision placeholders, terminology drift, or formatting/period-language violations.

Treat scene texture, hook strength, emotional residue, terminology support, life warmth, and market stickiness as diagnostic modules. Do not force every module into every chapter or turn all chapters into the same template.

## 11. Dynamic Audit Rule

Do not preserve old metrics as current truth. Recompute at each release review:

- chapter length and threshold counts;
- repeated sentence patterns and abstract-system vocabulary;
- unresolved setup and decision placeholders;
- first-use professional terms;
- author/meta residue;
- unfilled timeline or custody cells;
- rule conflicts and stale alternatives;
- working-tree or draft-version mismatches when reviewing a repository.

Date every audit result or label it as a snapshot. Historical feedback may stay in a change log, but current execution documents must not inherit stale counts or superseded choices.

## Review Output

For architecture review, report in this order:

1. overall architecture verdict;
2. hard continuity or rule blockers;
3. highest-yield structural improvements;
4. canonical decisions that should be fixed now;
5. open questions that can safely remain open;
6. implementation documents that must be synchronized;
7. verification checklist before正文 revision or release.

Do not solve architecture weaknesses by adding more mysteries, rules, factions, or terminology unless the existing story truly lacks a causal engine.
